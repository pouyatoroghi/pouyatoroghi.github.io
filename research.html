Research

My current research asks how to detect hidden behavior in language models and how to assess the correctness of their responses after fine-tuning. Earlier, I studied the removal of training-data influence. Below I distinguish my contributions to these projects from the questions I would like to pursue during a PhD.

Backdoor detection in large language models

Sept. 2024–present

M.Sc. research · RIML Lab, Sharif University of Technology

Problem. What can full access to a model reveal about a backdoor when the trigger distribution is unknown?

My contribution. I am developing a search-based detection method together with partial estimation of that distribution under explicit assumptions.

Evaluation. I study how white-box information can guide candidate-trigger discovery and support the identification of backdoored models. Evaluation under controlled attack settings examines detection performance, the usefulness of estimated trigger information, and the limitations imposed by the assumptions.

Advisor: Mohammad Hossein Rohban

Ongoing M.Sc. thesis · Manuscript in preparation for planned ICML submission

Calibration-aware fine-tuning

Dec. 2025–present

Remote research project with CUHK

Problem. This project studies the relationship between confidence and semantic correctness in long-form generation, especially after fine-tuning with limited data or under distribution shift.

My contribution. I contributed to the problem formulation and am developing training-time methods that use in-domain supervision to align confidence with empirical response correctness.

Evaluation. The work examines calibration, generation accuracy, and inference cost together. My contributions include method development, baseline comparisons, and the training and evaluation needed to assess these trade-offs. The aim is useful uncertainty estimation without relying on extensive inference-time sampling.

Advisor: Amin Gohari

Ongoing research · Manuscript in preparation for planned ICML submission

Machine unlearning for neural networks

Sept. 2023–Sept. 2024

Completed B.Sc. research · Sharif University of Technology

Problem and contribution. My undergraduate thesis examined the removal of training-data influence, including in distributed learning. I compared existing algorithms and developed and evaluated a federated unlearning approach.

Evaluation. I designed an empirical protocol to assess the effects of unlearning. I combined retained-task performance and forgetting measures with membership-inference and poisoning attacks before and after unlearning. These tests assess different properties: low attack success alone does not establish that the influence of the deleted data has been removed.

Advisors: Arash Amini and Stefano Rini

Directions for doctoral research

My closest starting points are uncertainty under adaptation, search with learned evaluators, and model revision. I am interested in theory-focused work that makes assumptions explicit and connects guarantees to observable behavior. The following are prospective questions, not claims of new results.

Uncertainty under adaptation

When fine-tuning changes both predictions and confidence, which reliability properties can be preserved with limited labels? My calibration project motivates studying the distinction between average calibration and the reliability of predictions selected for a downstream decision.

Search and sequential decisions

How should a search procedure allocate a fixed budget when its evaluator is imperfect? My work with program synthesis and search, and my RL preparation, motivate studying how evaluator error affects the probability of finding a valid solution.

Controlled model revision

How much information about training is needed to remove specified data influence without retraining from scratch? My unlearning work motivates comparisons against a retrained reference, separating distributional guarantees from empirical attack resistance.

Learning theory and optimization

How do restricted parameter updates change stability and generalization? Fine-tuning and unlearning provide concrete interventions to study, beginning with models whose optimization dynamics can be analyzed rather than assuming conclusions transfer immediately to large networks.

Statistical identification and security

Under a specified family of attacks, what can white-box access reveal that behavioral queries cannot? My thesis motivates characterizing the assumptions and search budget needed to distinguish a backdoor from benign rare behavior.

Representation and generative modeling

When does a representation preserve the information required for a new inference task? My signal-processing and latent-variable projects motivate studying this first in tractable observation models, where reconstruction quality and uncertainty can be evaluated separately.
