I study uncertainty and the reliability of learned models. My current work concerns backdoor detection in language models and semantic calibration after fine-tuning; my undergraduate thesis examined machine unlearning.

I am advised by Mohammad Hossein Rohban in the RIML Lab at Sharif, and work remotely with Amin Gohari on the CUHK calibration project. Both current projects have manuscripts in preparation for planned ICML submission.

My central interest is mathematical: what does the available information let a learner infer, and how should that uncertainty affect its next decision? I want to connect statistical inference with search and sequential decision-making, building on my electrical-engineering background.

Research interests

My closest PhD directions are uncertainty under adaptation, reasoning under limited computation, and controlled model revision. I am also interested in the learning-theoretic and optimization questions these problems raise. My research page sets out possible starting problems; my long-term questions explain the broader ambition toward general intelligence.

Selected mathematical exposition

What can historical decisions tell us?

A sequential estimator, its exact bias, and a two-environment lower bound for policy evaluation with poor coverage.

What does an adaptive experiment identify?

A matrix confidence bound and a sensor example showing why many measurements can leave a parameter contrast unknown.

These are worked expositions of established methods, with stated assumptions and reproducible model checks. All mathematical notes.
