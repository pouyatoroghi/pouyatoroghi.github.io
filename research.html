Mohammad Pouya Toroghi

Research

My research examines properties of learned models that require different kinds of evidence: the presence of a backdoor, the reliability of a confidence estimate, and the removal of training-data influence. The shared concern is what an experiment can establish about a model, and what remains outside its assumptions.

Backdoor detection in large language models

Sept. 2024–present

M.Sc. research · RIML Lab, Sharif University of Technology

My master's research asks how full access to a model can support backdoor detection when the trigger distribution is not known in advance. I am developing a search-based method that uses white-box access to guide candidate-trigger discovery, together with partial estimation of the trigger distribution under explicit assumptions.

The experimental work examines both detection performance and the value of the estimated trigger information. I evaluate model responses under controlled attack settings to understand which information helps identify a backdoored model and where the assumptions limit the approach.

Advisor: Mohammad Hossein Rohban

Calibration-aware fine-tuning

Dec. 2025–present

Remote research project with CUHK

This project studies confidence in long-form language generation after fine-tuning, particularly when data are limited or the evaluation distribution changes. I contributed to the problem formulation and am developing training-time methods that use in-domain supervision to align confidence with empirical response correctness.

My work includes training and evaluation pipelines, baseline comparisons, and analysis of calibration–accuracy and inference-cost trade-offs. The aim is to obtain useful uncertainty estimates while preserving generation quality and limiting inference overhead. I evaluate these objectives together so that a calibration improvement is considered alongside the quality and cost of the generated responses.

Advisor: Amin Gohari

Ongoing work · Manuscript in preparation

Machine unlearning for neural networks

Sept. 2023–Sept. 2024

Completed B.Sc. research · Sharif University of Technology

My undergraduate thesis examined the removal of training-data influence from neural networks, including distributed training. I implemented and compared existing methods and developed and evaluated a federated unlearning approach.

Evaluation was a substantial part of the work. I combined retained-task performance and forgetting measures with membership-inference and poisoning attacks, assessing models before and after unlearning. This let me examine utility, privacy, and security as distinct dimensions of a method, and assess its effectiveness through several forms of evidence.

Advisors: Arash Amini and Stefano Rini
