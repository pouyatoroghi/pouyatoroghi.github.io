Research

My research connects questions about inference, uncertainty, and the effects of modifying a learned model. Current projects concern language-model security and calibration; earlier work examined machine unlearning. I am interested in the assumptions that make these problems tractable and in the limits of the evidence available to answer them.

Backdoor detection in large language models

Sept. 2024–present

M.Sc. research · RIML Lab, Sharif University of Technology

The starting point of my master's research is an inference problem: what can full access to a model reveal about a backdoor when the trigger distribution is unknown? I am developing a search-based detection method together with partial estimation of that distribution under explicit assumptions.

I study how white-box information can guide candidate-trigger discovery and support the identification of backdoored models. Evaluation under controlled attack settings examines detection performance, the usefulness of estimated trigger information, and the limitations imposed by the assumptions.

Advisor: Mohammad Hossein Rohban

Calibration-aware fine-tuning

Dec. 2025–present

Remote research project with CUHK

This project studies the relationship between confidence and semantic correctness in long-form generation, especially after fine-tuning with limited data or under distribution shift. I contributed to the problem formulation and am developing training-time methods that use in-domain supervision to align confidence with empirical response correctness.

The work examines calibration, generation accuracy, and inference cost together. My contributions include method development, baseline comparisons, and the training and evaluation needed to assess these trade-offs. The aim is useful uncertainty estimation without relying on extensive inference-time sampling.

Advisor: Amin Gohari

Machine unlearning for neural networks

Sept. 2023–Sept. 2024

Completed B.Sc. research · Sharif University of Technology

My undergraduate thesis examined the removal of training-data influence, including in distributed learning. I compared existing algorithms and developed and evaluated a federated unlearning approach.

A central part of the work was specifying how its effects should be assessed. I combined retained-task performance and forgetting measures with membership-inference and poisoning attacks before and after unlearning. This treated utility, privacy, and security as distinct criteria, providing several forms of evidence about a method's effectiveness and limitations.

Advisors: Arash Amini and Stefano Rini

Directions for doctoral research

I want to pursue mathematical questions across learning and AI, using experiments to test and refine the analysis. These are prospective directions connected by my preparation in electrical engineering, inference, and decision-making; I expect the specific problems to develop through collaboration during a PhD.

Learning theory and optimization

I want to study how problem structure and the optimization procedure influence generalization. Stability, regularization, data efficiency, and the effects of training interventions interest me across model classes. My calibration and unlearning research provide concrete settings in which to connect mathematical analysis with experiments that test the relevance of its assumptions.

Probabilistic inference and uncertainty

I want to understand what a model's uncertainty measures and when that information remains useful under distribution shift or scarce feedback. My calibration research, Bayesian-network inference, and mixture-model projects connect to this interest. I am particularly drawn to statistical guarantees whose assumptions reflect the information actually available to the learner.

Reinforcement learning and sequential decisions

Value estimation, exploration, and planning connect my signals-and-systems background with my theoretical study of reinforcement learning. I want to study how approximation error and uncertainty affect decisions over time, and how limits on data and computation should influence a learning strategy. My Deep Reinforcement Learning teaching and projects provide a foundation for this direction.

Reasoning and structured representations

I want to connect learned representations with search, constraints, and executable structure. Program synthesis, symbolic regression, and constraint solving have given me different perspectives on this connection. I would like to study how abstraction and intermediate evaluation affect compositional generalization and the resources needed to solve an unfamiliar problem.

Representation learning and generative modeling

I am interested in what structure representations capture and which information learning objectives preserve. PCA, expectation-maximization, autoencoders, variational models, and diffusion provide several starting points. I want to study connections among latent-variable inference, optimization, and generalization, including questions motivated by signals, speech, and vision.

Robustness and principled model revision

My security and unlearning work motivate questions about identifying hidden failures and changing model behavior in a controlled way. I want to study what can be inferred from model access, which guarantees a specified threat model permits, and how interventions affect utility, privacy, and security. This also connects to my interest in explanations that can be tested against model behavior.
