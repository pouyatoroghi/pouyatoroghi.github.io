Evaluating a new policy from old trajectories

Applied probability · Worked analysis

A new recommendation policy must be assessed using sessions collected by an older policy. The derivation below builds an estimator, calculates precisely how model errors enter its bias, and proves when even a large log cannot answer the question.

Problem and assumptionsExact bias derivationA coverage lower bound

1. The decision hidden inside the logs

A recommendation system has recorded sequences of recommendations, user responses, and subsequent states. A proposed policy changes which actions are taken. Before deploying it, the question is whether its expected cumulative reward is higher. A mean of the logged rewards answers a different question: how well the logging policy performed.

Model one session as a finite-horizon Markov trajectory with times t=0,…,H−1t=0,\ldots,H-1t=0,…,H−1, a finite action set, bounded rewards, and a common initial-state law. The behavior policy bt(a∣s)b_t(a\mid s)bt​(a∣s) generated the logs; the fixed target policy is πt(a∣s)\pi_t(a\mid s)πt​(a∣s). Conditional on state and action, the joint law of the next state and reward is the same under both policies. This invariance is substantive: unrecorded factors that affect action choice and outcomes can invalidate the model.

vπ=Eπ ⁣[∑t=0H−1Rt].v^\pi=\mathbb E_\pi\!\bigl[\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}R_t\bigr].vπ=Eπ​[t=0∑H−1​Rt​].

Assume overlap: whenever the target assigns positive probability to an action at a target-reachable state, the behavior policy does too. For the finite-moment arguments below, assume the relevant probability ratios are uniformly bounded. Use independent sessions for evaluation, and fit any value functions or estimated behavior probabilities on separate data. All derivations can then be read conditional on those fitted objects.

The sequential doubly robust construction is established; see Jiang and Li (2016). Here the focus is the complete cancellation argument, the exact bias when both fitted components are wrong, and a concrete coverage obstruction.

2. Derive the trajectory likelihood ratio

Write the shared transition-and-reward kernel as Kt(dst+1,drt∣st,at)K_t(ds_{t+1},dr_t\mid s_t,a_t)Kt​(dst+1​,drt​∣st​,at​). Up to time ttt, a behavior trajectory has density or probability mass proportional to

μ(ds0)∏k=0tbk(ak∣sk) Kk(dsk+1,drk∣sk,ak).\mu(ds_0)\prod_{k=0}^{t}b_k(a_k\mid s_k)\,K_k(ds_{k+1},dr_k\mid s_k,a_k).μ(ds0​)k=0∏t​bk​(ak​∣sk​)Kk​(dsk+1​,drk​∣sk​,ak​).

Replacing each behavior probability by its target counterpart leaves every kernel and the initial law unchanged. They cancel in the ratio, giving

wt=∏k=0tπk(Ak∣Sk)bk(Ak∣Sk),w−1=1.w_t=\prod_{k=0}^{t}\frac{\pi_k(A_k\mid S_k)}{b_k(A_k\mid S_k)},\qquad w_{-1}=1.wt​=k=0∏t​bk​(Ak​∣Sk​)πk​(Ak​∣Sk​)​,w−1​=1.

Thus, for any integrable function of the trajectory through the outcome at time ttt, multiplying by wtw_twt​ changes its expectation from behavior to target. In particular,

Eb[wtRt]=Eπ[Rt].\mathbb E_b[w_tR_t]=\mathbb E_\pi[R_t].Eb​[wt​Rt​]=Eπ​[Rt​].

This proves unbiasedness of per-decision importance sampling. It also exposes its weakness: the product can become enormous on the few trajectories that resemble the target policy.

3. Add a model without trusting it completely

Let q^t(s,a)\widehat q_t(s,a)q​t​(s,a) be any bounded fitted action-value function. Define its target-policy average and its terminal value by

v^t(s)=∑aπt(a∣s)q^t(s,a),v^H=0.\widehat v_t(s)=\sum_a\pi_t(a\mid s)\widehat q_t(s,a),\qquad \widehat v_H=0.vt​(s)=a∑​πt​(a∣s)q​t​(s,a),vH​=0.

The corrected estimate from a single trajectory is

D=v^0(S0)+∑t=0H−1wt[Rt+v^t+1(St+1)−q^t(St,At)].D=\widehat v_0(S_0)+\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}w_t\bigl[R_t+\widehat v_{t+1}(S_{t+1})-\widehat q_t(S_t,A_t)\bigr].D=v0​(S0​)+t=0∑H−1​wt​[Rt​+vt+1​(St+1​)−q​t​(St​,At​)].

To see the cancellation, condition immediately before the action is drawn. The previous weight is already known, so

Eb[wtq^t(St,At)∣past,St]=wt−1v^t(St).\mathbb E_b[w_t\widehat q_t(S_t,A_t)\mid\text{past},S_t]=w_{t-1}\widehat v_t(S_t).Eb​[wt​q​t​(St​,At​)∣past,St​]=wt−1​vt​(St​).

Indeed, the conditional sum over actions contains bt(a∣St)b_t(a\mid S_t)bt​(a∣St​) times πt(a∣St)/bt(a∣St)\pi_t(a\mid S_t)/b_t(a\mid S_t)πt​(a∣St​)/bt​(a∣St​); the behavior probabilities cancel. Taking expectations, the negative term at time ttt cancels the positive fitted-value term carried from time t−1t-1t−1. The initial fitted value cancels the time-zero negative term, and the final fitted value is zero. Only the weighted rewards remain:

Eb[D]=∑t=0H−1Eb[wtRt]=vπ.\mathbb E_b[D]=\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\mathbb E_b[w_tR_t]=v^\pi.Eb​[D]=t=0∑H−1​Eb​[wt​Rt​]=vπ.

The model may be misspecified. With correct behavior probabilities, its error changes the variance, but not this expectation. The sample mean of DDD over independent evaluation sessions estimates the desired value.

4. When both components are fitted: an exact bias identity

Replace the behavior probabilities in the weights by positive estimates b^t\widehat b_tbt​, obtaining w^t\widehat w_twt​ and an estimator D^\widehat DD. Define the true target value functions through the Bellman equations

Qtπ(s,a)=E[Rt+Vt+1π(St+1)∣St=s,At=a],Q_t^\pi(s,a)=\mathbb E[R_t+V_{t+1}^\pi(S_{t+1})\mid S_t=s,A_t=a],Qtπ​(s,a)=E[Rt​+Vt+1π​(St+1​)∣St​=s,At​=a],
Vtπ(s)=∑aπt(a∣s)Qtπ(s,a),VHπ=0.V_t^\pi(s)=\sum_a\pi_t(a\mid s)Q_t^\pi(s,a),\qquad V_H^\pi=0.Vtπ​(s)=a∑​πt​(a∣s)Qtπ​(s,a),VHπ​=0.

Separate the fitted-value error and its action average:

et=q^t−Qtπ,eˉt(s)=∑aπt(a∣s)et(s,a).e_t=\widehat q_t-Q_t^\pi,\qquad \bar e_t(s)=\sum_a\pi_t(a\mid s)e_t(s,a).et​=q​t​−Qtπ​,eˉt​(s)=a∑​πt​(a∣s)et​(s,a).

The true Bellman residual is

εt=Rt+Vt+1π(St+1)−Qtπ(St,At).\varepsilon_t=R_t+V_{t+1}^\pi(S_{t+1})-Q_t^\pi(S_t,A_t).εt​=Rt​+Vt+1π​(St+1​)−Qtπ​(St​,At​).

It has zero conditional mean given the history through the current action. The weight w^t\widehat w_twt​ is measurable at that point, hence Eb[w^tεt]=0\mathbb E_b[\widehat w_t\varepsilon_t]=0Eb​[wt​εt​]=0. Substituting the error decomposition into the estimator and taking expectations leaves

Eb[D^]−vπ=Eb ⁣[eˉ0(S0)+∑t=0H−1w^t(eˉt+1(St+1)−et(St,At))].\mathbb E_b[\widehat D]-v^\pi=\mathbb E_b\!\bigl[\bar e_0(S_0)+\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\widehat w_t\bigl(\bar e_{t+1}(S_{t+1})-e_t(S_t,A_t)\bigr)\bigr].Eb​[D]−vπ=Eb​[eˉ0​(S0​)+t=0∑H−1​wt​(eˉt+1​(St+1​)−et​(St​,At​))].

Shift the index of the positive terms, using w^−1=1\widehat w_{-1}=1w−1​=1 and eˉH=0\bar e_H=0eˉH​=0. The same expression becomes

∑t=0H−1Eb ⁣[w^t−1eˉt(St)−w^tet(St,At)].\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\mathbb E_b\!\bigl[\widehat w_{t-1}\bar e_t(S_t)-\widehat w_te_t(S_t,A_t)\bigr].t=0∑H−1​Eb​[wt−1​eˉt​(St​)−wt​et​(St​,At​)].

Now condition on the pre-action history. The second term averages under the actual behavior policy, not its estimate. This gives the exact product-error formula

Eb[D^]−vπ=∑t=0H−1Eb[w^t−1Bt(St)],\mathbb E_b[\widehat D]-v^\pi=\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\mathbb E_b[\widehat w_{t-1}B_t(S_t)],Eb​[D]−vπ=t=0∑H−1​Eb​[wt−1​Bt​(St​)],
Bt(s)=∑aπt(a∣s)et(s,a)[1−bt(a∣s)b^t(a∣s)].B_t(s)=\sum_a\pi_t(a\mid s)e_t(s,a)\bigl[1-\frac{b_t(a\mid s)}{\widehat b_t(a\mid s)}\bigr].Bt​(s)=a∑​πt​(a∣s)et​(s,a)[1−bt​(a∣s)bt​(a∣s)​].

If all fitted behavior probabilities are correct, the bracket vanishes. If all fitted action values are correct, the value error vanishes. Either condition yields unbiasedness. If both are wrong, their errors multiply locally, but cumulative weights still amplify the result. “Doubly robust” therefore does not mean immune to weak overlap, or unbiased whenever each model is merely close.

5. Identify the variance that remains with a perfect value model

Take correct weights and, for this calculation, q^t=Qtπ\widehat q_t=Q_t^\piq​t​=Qtπ​. Then

D=V0π(S0)+∑t=0H−1wtεt.D=V_0^\pi(S_0)+\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}w_t\varepsilon_t.D=V0π​(S0​)+t=0∑H−1​wt​εt​.

For s<ts<ts<t, the earlier weighted residual is known before the time-ttt outcome. Conditioning there makes the later residual's mean zero, so cross terms vanish. The initial value is orthogonal to every residual for the same reason. Define

σt2(s,a)=Var⁡(Rt+Vt+1π(St+1)∣St=s,At=a).\sigma_t^2(s,a)=\operatorname{Var}(R_t+V_{t+1}^\pi(S_{t+1})\mid S_t=s,A_t=a).σt2​(s,a)=Var(Rt​+Vt+1π​(St+1​)∣St​=s,At​=a).

Expanding the square now gives

Var⁡b(D)=Var⁡(V0π(S0))+∑t=0H−1Eb[wt2σt2(St,At)].\operatorname{Var}_b(D)=\operatorname{Var}(V_0^\pi(S_0))+\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\mathbb E_b[w_t^2\sigma_t^2(S_t,A_t)].Varb​(D)=Var(V0π​(S0​))+t=0∑H−1​Eb​[wt2​σt2​(St​,At​)].

For NNN independent sessions, divide this variance by NNN. This is a best-model benchmark for this estimator, not a claim that the true value function is available. Even the benchmark retains transition and reward noise multiplied by squared likelihood ratios. A better regression model alone cannot remove that factor.

6. A recommendation sequence for which data are fundamentally insufficient

Consider a session with HHH binary choices. A terminal conversion is possible only if every action is one; any zero moves the system into an absorbing failure state. The state records whether all previous choices succeeded, so the process is Markov. The behavior policy chooses one with probability ppp at each step. The target always chooses one. Conditional on reaching the terminal success state, the conversion is Bernoulli with unknown mean ϑ\varthetaϑ. The target value is exactly ϑ\varthetaϑ.

Let EEE denote the all-one trajectory. Its behavior probability is pHp^HpH. The importance-weighted return is zero off this trajectory and equals p−HBp^{-H}Bp−HB on it, where B∼Bernoulli⁡(ϑ)B\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\vartheta)B∼Bernoulli(ϑ). Direct calculation gives

Eb[wH−1B1E]=ϑ,Eb[(wH−1B1E)2]=ϑp−H,\mathbb E_b[w_{H-1}B\mathbf1_E]=\vartheta,\qquad \mathbb E_b[(w_{H-1}B\mathbf1_E)^2]=\vartheta p^{-H},Eb​[wH−1​B1E​]=ϑ,Eb​[(wH−1​B1E​)2]=ϑp−H,
Var⁡b(wH−1B1E)=ϑp−H−ϑ2.\operatorname{Var}_b(w_{H-1}B\mathbf1_E)=\vartheta p^{-H}-\vartheta^2.Varb​(wH−1​B1E​)=ϑp−H−ϑ2.

The difficulty is not peculiar to importance sampling. Compare two environments with ϑ=0\vartheta=0ϑ=0 and ϑ=1\vartheta=1ϑ=1. If none of NNN logged sessions reaches EEE, their observed data have identical distributions. That event has probability

qN=(1−pH)N.q_N=(1-p^H)^N.qN​=(1−pH)N.

On the shared data, any estimate zzz satisfies ∣z∣+∣z−1∣≥1|z|+|z-1|\ge1∣z∣+∣z−1∣≥1. Couple the two environments to have the same action paths and the same estimator randomness. Integrating this inequality over the no-coverage event gives, for every estimator ϑ^\widehat\varthetaϑ,

max⁡ϑ∈{0,1}Eϑ∣ϑ^−ϑ∣≥(1−pH)N2.\max_{\vartheta\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_\vartheta|\widehat\vartheta-\vartheta|\ge\frac{(1-p^H)^N}{2}.ϑ∈{0,1}max​Eϑ​∣ϑ−ϑ∣≥2(1−pH)N​.

With p=1/2p=1/2p=1/2, H=20H=20H=20, and N=100,000N=100{,}000N=100,000, the chance of seeing no informative trajectory is about 0.9090. The worst-case expected absolute error is therefore at least 0.4545. This lower bound assumes no additional structural knowledge or separately collected informative outcomes. It explains why a large log can still contain almost no evidence for a proposed policy.

7. A finite model check and the practical consequence

The accompanying check enumerates all 128 trajectories of a two-stage, two-state model, including binary rewards and stochastic state transitions. The exact target value is 1.132665. A misspecified value model with correct action probabilities, or a correct value model with misspecified probabilities, returns that same expectation. With both perturbed, the expectation is 1.162530895; the product-error identity independently gives the difference 0.029865895. The oracle variance decomposition also agrees with direct enumeration.

These are checks on a specified finite model, not results from a deployed recommender. For an actual log, the first questions are whether the state makes the outcome law invariant across policies, whether action probabilities were recorded correctly, and whether the target's important trajectories have coverage. The proof says exactly why each matters. Clipping weights may reduce variance, but it changes the expectation and requires its own bias analysis.
