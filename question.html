Long-term research questions

Ambitions I want to pursue through doctoral research and beyond

My long-term ambition is to contribute to artificial general intelligence by understanding how a system can assess, extend, and revise its own reasoning. I use search over partial solutions as a working perspective on this problem, rather than assuming it is a complete account of intelligence.

The four questions below connect that ambition to bounded problems I would like to investigate. They are research interests, not established solutions or claims of novelty.

What mathematical principles let systems reason, generalize, and revise their knowledge reliably?

Reasoning as search under limited computation

Can general-purpose reasoning become a principled allocation of limited computation? I view reasoning as search over partial solutions. I want to understand how calibrated assessments of intermediate states can guide that search, distinguishing local validity, the probability of eventual success, and the expected benefit of further computation.

A first setting I would like to study is program synthesis with an execution-based verifier and a fixed search budget. A useful evaluator must distinguish promising branches, not merely be calibrated on average: a constant base-rate forecast can be calibrated and still give no ranking. I would compare learned guidance with unguided search and fixed heuristics at matched evaluation cost, measuring valid solutions and failure cases. The continuation policy and remaining budget must be specified when defining a branch's probability of success.

Abstractions that support new discoveries

Can a system learn abstractions that remain useful when the problem changes? A mathematical abstraction can expose common structure across problems that initially look unrelated. I want to understand how learning systems might discover similarly reusable representations and compose them in settings beyond their training experience.

My program-synthesis and symbolic-regression projects suggest a starting point: learn a library of reusable components, then test it on held-out compositions. I would compare it with a fixed library at matched search cost and account for the cost of learning the library. Transfer to genuinely new compositions, and failures under changed primitives, would help distinguish useful abstraction from retrieval of familiar solutions. The longer-term aim is systems that assist mathematical and scientific discovery.

Reliable learning in a changing world

Can a learning system remain statistically trustworthy as its data, decisions, and behavior change? I want to study uncertainty estimates that remain useful through distribution shifts, adaptation, and self-directed data collection. My semantic-calibration research provides a starting point; my security work makes hidden failures a particular concern.

I would begin with a specified source of shift and a limited stream of correctness labels. The question is how feedback frequency constrains a decision-level guarantee, such as error among accepted answers. Calibration, prediction-set coverage, and selective error are different targets; success on one does not establish the others. The ambition requires knowing when reliability is unattainable as well as designing methods where the assumptions permit it.

Learning that can revise and forget

Can a system learn continuously while preserving control over what it retains, revises, and forgets? My unlearning research motivates a broader goal: systems that incorporate new evidence, correct harmful behavior, and remove specified data influence while preserving useful knowledge.

A first problem is to characterize deletion in a tractable learning model under explicit limits on stored training information. I would compare the updated model with retraining on the retained data, measuring both predictive utility and the relevant distributional discrepancy. In federated settings, communication and retained client state add constraints. Passing a membership-inference test is useful evidence, but cannot by itself certify equivalence to retraining.

How I want to pursue these questions

My PhD directions identify where my current preparation gives me a starting point. I want to develop precise models, prove what they permit or rule out, and test whether their assumptions explain observed behavior. I expect the questions to change through collaboration and evidence; the ambition is long-term, while a useful first result may be deliberately narrow.

Related reading

Mathematical and methodological starting points for these interests:

  • Bousquet and Elisseeff (2002). Stability and Generalization. Connections between algorithmic stability and generalization.
  • Hay et al. (2012). Selecting Computations: Theory and Applications. Metareasoning and the value of computation.
  • Guo et al. (2017). On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks. Probability calibration and its evaluation.
  • Gibbs and Candès (2021). Adaptive Conformal Inference Under Distribution Shift. Adaptive prediction-set coverage, distinct from conditional calibration.
  • Ellis et al. (2021). DreamCoder: Bootstrapping Inductive Program Synthesis with Wake-Sleep Library Learning. Learning reusable program abstractions and search policies.
  • Ginart et al. (2019). Making AI Forget You: Data Deletion in Machine Learning. Formal data deletion and algorithms for specific learning settings.
  • Kingma and Welling (2013). Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes. Variational inference and learning in latent-variable models.
