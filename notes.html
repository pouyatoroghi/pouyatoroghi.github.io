Mathematical notes

Probability problems that arise when evaluating a new policy, measuring rare failures, or deciding what to measure next. These notes work through the model, the difficult steps of the derivation, and the conditions under which the answer is useful.

The analyses use established methods, cited in each note. Worked examples and counterexamples make the assumptions concrete; numerical checks are reproducible. Earlier notes cover supporting results, and a separate entry presents a calculation from my handwritten notebook.

Applied probability problems

Models, proofs, and limits

01

Evaluating a new policy from old trajectories

A recommendation policy changes the distribution of entire sessions. Derive a corrected value estimator, its exact product-error bias and martingale variance, then prove a lower bound showing when the logs cannot identify the new policy's value.

Trajectory change of measure · Double robustness · Two-environment lower bound

02

Rare false alarms in quadratic detectors

Estimate a false-alarm probability near one in a hundred million. Derive the Gaussian covariance tilt and exact variance, then use an endpoint Laplace argument to prove the remaining simulation cost, including its polynomial factor.

Quadratic forms · Exponential tilting · Exact second moments · Tail asymptotics

03

Sensor calibration with adaptive measurements

The next excitation depends on earlier observations. Derive a matrix-valued confidence guarantee that respects this dependence, connect its determinant to experimental information, and show why repeated measurements can leave a coefficient contrast unidentified.

Self-normalized martingales · Matrix Gaussian integration · Experimental design
