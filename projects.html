Mohammad Pouya Toroghi

Selected studies and projects

These projects reflect the mathematical ideas I have worked with across machine learning and AI: inference over hidden variables, search over discrete structures, estimation of long-term value, and learning useful representations. Implementation and experiments have been ways to study these ideas in concrete settings.

Probabilistic inference and latent-variable models

I worked with inference in Bayesian networks using variable elimination and sampling, and with Gaussian mixture models fitted by expectation-maximization for medical-image analysis. These projects involve different ways of reasoning about unobserved variables: exploiting conditional structure, approximating probabilities, and estimating latent components from data.

My representation and restoration projects also included PCA-based reconstruction and neural autoencoders for denoising, colorization, and super-resolution. They connect linear-algebraic representations with learned nonlinear models and their behavior on reconstruction tasks.

Search, constraints, and structured reasoning

My classical AI work covered graph search, including A*, local and adversarial search, and constraint solving through backtracking, AC-3/AC-4, and CP-SAT. These methods connect reasoning about discrete possibilities with the use of structure to reduce a search space.

In program synthesis and neuro-symbolic projects, I worked with program generation and execution-based evaluation, symbolic regression, and reasoning methods including Tree of Thoughts and Monte Carlo tree search. I also built graph-based and retrieval-augmented reasoning pipelines. The connection that interests me is how representations, constraints, and evaluation shape the search for a solution.

Sequential decision-making and reinforcement learning

My reinforcement learning projects combined theoretical study with empirical evaluation. They span Monte Carlo and temporal-difference methods, Q-learning and SARSA, policy-gradient methods, and deep value-based and actor-critic algorithms such as DQN, PPO, SAC, and DDPG.

I extended this work to imitation learning, offline RL, multi-agent learning, hierarchical control, and exploration. The mathematical connections I want to pursue further concern value estimation, policy optimization, and learning from data whose distribution depends on the decisions an agent makes.

Representation learning and generative modeling

I worked with variational autoencoders, DDPMs, and latent diffusion, alongside models for vision, language, and temporal prediction. These projects exposed me to latent-variable modeling and iterative generation, and to different ways learned representations can support prediction and reconstruction.

Related work included LoRA and prefix tuning, representation learning, and attention visualization in vision-language models. I am interested in carrying this experience toward questions about what structure representations preserve, how they change during adaptation, and when they transfer to new tasks.

Signals, language, and generalization across conditions

I developed a Persian speech recognition system using a Conformer-Transducer architecture, with evaluation across speakers and acoustic conditions. The project connected my signals background to language modeling and to the practical consequences of variation in observed data.

Other work included text classification with bag-of-words and TF-IDF representations, classical classifiers, and Transformers. These applications complement my interests in inference and representation by providing concrete settings in which to examine the usefulness of a learned model.
