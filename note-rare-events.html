Rare false alarms in quadratic detectors

Applied probability · Worked analysis

A residual-energy detector may need a false-alarm probability so small that ordinary simulation is impractical. Starting from the Gaussian density, this analysis constructs a different sampling law, derives its exact variance in a calibration case, and proves how its cost scales as alarms become rarer.

The detector problemExact variance comparisonTail and efficiency proof

Result and scope. Under a specified Gaussian model, covariance tilting gives an unbiased rare-event estimator. In the spherical case with a fixed threshold multiplier, its relative second moment grows only as the square root of dimension: logarithmic efficiency, but not bounded relative error.

1. A detector whose failures ordinary simulation almost never sees

A monitoring system raises an alarm when a weighted residual energy exceeds a threshold. Under nominal operation, suppose the residual vector is centered Gaussian with known positive-definite covariance. If the covariance is singular, first restrict to its known support and let the dimension below be the support dimension. Whitening gives Z∼N(0,In)Z\sim\mathcal N(0,I_n)Z∼N(0,In​); the alarm statistic becomes

T=Z⊤AZ,A⪰0,A≠0.T=Z^\top A Z,\qquad A\succeq0,\quad A\ne0.T=Z⊤AZ,A⪰0,A=0.

The target is pc=Pr⁡(T≥c)p_c=\Pr(T\ge c)pc​=Pr(T≥c) for c>tr⁡(A)c>\operatorname{tr}(A)c>tr(A). The weights may emphasize particular frequency bands or sensor directions. A direct Monte Carlo estimate averages alarm indicators. With mmm independent draws, its relative mean-square error is

E[(p^c−pc)2]pc2=1−pcmpc.\frac{\mathbb E[(\widehat p_c-p_c)^2]}{p_c^2}=\frac{1-p_c}{mp_c}.pc2​E[(p​c​−pc​)2]​=mpc​1−pc​​.

A probability near 10−810^{-8}10−8 therefore requires roughly ten billion draws for 10% relative root-mean-square error. The task is to make alarms common in simulation while correcting exactly for the changed distribution. The method is exponential tilting; Owen, Chapter 9, Sections 9.1 and 9.6, provides background. Here the Gaussian change of covariance, its exact finite-dimensional variance, and the asymptotic cost are derived explicitly.

It does not have bounded relative error: the ratio still grows. These are sample-count statements; generating an nnn-dimensional sample also has a computational cost.

For a weighted detector the exact chi-square simplification usually disappears, while the determinant formula and covariance tilt remain available. The fixed-aaa efficiency proof above does not automatically extend to every sequence of weight matrices. A spectrum dominated by a few directions can change the rare-event geometry.

7. What the calculation certifies

This separates a simulation problem from a modeling problem. Exponential tilting can estimate a tiny probability accurately under a specified distribution. It cannot make that distribution correct. Estimated whitening, parameter fitting on the same residuals, a nonzero residual mean, or heavy tails change the nominal false-alarm law.

The general calculation supplies a proposal when exact tail evaluation is unavailable. The spherical case provides a stringent benchmark: normalization, unbiasedness, exact variance, and asymptotic cost can all be checked independently.
