Confidence intervals that survive repeated inspection

Sequential inference · Expository derivation

An experiment rarely ends at a sample size chosen with complete indifference to its results. This note derives a confidence sequence that remains valid when observations determine when to stop, and shows how to use it when evaluating a fixed model on fresh data.

The target and the conditional assumption

Suppose observations arrive one at a time, and we want an interval for their common mean that remains valid however long we keep looking. A confidence sequence makes a simultaneous statement over all sample sizes. The stopping rule can then depend on the observed data.

Let (Xt)t≥1(X_t)_{t\ge1}(Xt​)t≥1​ be real-valued and adapted to a filtration (Ft)t≥0(\mathcal F_t)_{t\ge0}(Ft​)t≥0​, representing the information available after each observation. Assume a fixed mean μ\muμ and a known sub-Gaussian variance proxy σ2>0\sigma^2>0σ2>0. Write Yt=Xt−μY_t=X_t-\muYt​=Xt​−μ and use Et−1\mathbb E_{t-1}Et−1​ for expectation conditional on Ft−1\mathcal F_{t-1}Ft−1​. The assumption is

Et−1eλYt≤eλ2σ2/2\mathbb E_{t-1}e^{\lambda Y_t}\allowbreak\le e^{\lambda^2\sigma^2/2}Et−1​eλYt​≤eλ2σ2/2

almost surely, for every time t≥1t\ge1t≥1 and every λ∈R\lambda\in\mathbb Rλ∈R. Independence is sufficient in familiar examples but is not required. The conditional assumption is what matters; a bound on the ordinary variance alone does not imply it. Define

St=∑s=1tYs,Vt=tσ2,S_t=\sum_{s=1}^{t}Y_s,\allowbreak\qquad V_t=t\sigma^2,St​=s=1∑t​Ys​,Vt​=tσ2,
Xˉt=1t∑s=1tXs.\bar X_t=\frac1t\sum_{s=1}^{t}X_s.Xˉt​=t1​s=1∑t​Xs​.

Fix a failure probability δ∈(0,1)\delta\in(0,1)δ∈(0,1) and a tuning parameter ρ>0\rho>0ρ>0 before inspecting the evaluation data. The construction below is the two-sided normal-mixture boundary in Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe and Sekhon, Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences, published in 2021; Appendix A.3, Proposition 5 of the linked revision gives the boundary.

1. Turn the moment bound into a supermartingale

For a fixed real λ\lambdaλ, consider the exponential process

Mtλ=eλSt−λ2Vt/2.M_t^\lambda=e^{\lambda S_t-\lambda^2V_t/2}.Mtλ​=eλSt​−λ2Vt​/2.

Separating the latest increment from the past gives

Et−1[Mtλ]=Mt−1λe−λ2σ2/2Et−1eλYt.\mathbb E_{t-1}[M_t^\lambda]\allowbreak=M_{t-1}^\lambda\allowbreak e^{-\lambda^2\sigma^2/2}\allowbreak\mathbb E_{t-1}e^{\lambda Y_t}.Et−1​[Mtλ​]=Mt−1λ​e−λ2σ2/2Et−1​eλYt​.

The assumed moment bound therefore implies

Et−1[Mtλ]≤Mt−1λ.\mathbb E_{t-1}[M_t^\lambda]\allowbreak\le M_{t-1}^\lambda.Et−1​[Mtλ​]≤Mt−1λ​.

Thus each process is a nonnegative supermartingale starting at one. Its expectation never exceeds one. Positive and negative values of the parameter respond to deviations in opposite directions; fixing one value would privilege a particular scale of deviation.

2. Mix without choosing a parameter from the data

Average the processes using the fixed distribution π=N(0,1/ρ)\pi=N(0,1/\rho)π=N(0,1/ρ):

Mt=∫RMtλ π(dλ).M_t=\int_{\mathbb R}M_t^\lambda\,\pi(d\lambda).Mt​=∫R​Mtλ​π(dλ).

The integrand is jointly measurable, being continuous in the parameter and measurable in the observations. Its nonnegativity permits conditional Tonelli:

Et−1[Mt]=∫REt−1[Mtλ] π(dλ),\mathbb E_{t-1}[M_t]\allowbreak=\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbb E_{t-1}[M_t^\lambda]\,\pi(d\lambda),Et−1​[Mt​]=∫R​Et−1​[Mtλ​]π(dλ),
Et−1[Mt]≤∫RMt−1λ π(dλ)=Mt−1.\mathbb E_{t-1}[M_t]\allowbreak\le\int_{\mathbb R}M_{t-1}^\lambda\,\pi(d\lambda)\allowbreak=M_{t-1}.Et−1​[Mt​]≤∫R​Mt−1λ​π(dλ)=Mt−1​.

Since M0=1M_0=1M0​=1, ordinary Tonelli also gives E[Mt]≤1\mathbb E[M_t]\le1E[Mt​]≤1, ensuring integrability. The mixture is therefore a nonnegative supermartingale. The Gaussian distribution specifies how the exponential processes are averaged; the observations themselves need not be Gaussian.

3. Evaluate the Gaussian mixture exactly

Set At=ρ+VtA_t=\rho+V_tAt​=ρ+Vt​. Substituting the normal density gives

Mt=ρ2π∫ReλSt−Atλ2/2 dλ.M_t=\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{2\pi}}\allowbreak\int_{\mathbb R}e^{\lambda S_t-A_t\lambda^2/2}\,d\lambda.Mt​=2πρ​​∫R​eλSt​−At​λ2/2dλ.

The useful algebraic step is completion of the square:

λSt−Atλ22=−At2(λ−StAt)2+St22At.\lambda S_t-\frac{A_t\lambda^2}{2}\allowbreak=-\frac{A_t}{2}\left(\lambda-\frac{S_t}{A_t}\right)^2\allowbreak+\frac{S_t^2}{2A_t}.λSt​−2At​λ2​=−2At​​(λ−At​St​​)2+2At​St2​​.

The centered Gaussian factor integrates to 2π/At\sqrt{2\pi/A_t}2π/At​​, leaving

Mt=ρAtexp⁡ ⁣(St22At).M_t=\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{A_t}}\allowbreak\exp\!\left(\frac{S_t^2}{2A_t}\right).Mt​=At​ρ​​exp(2At​St2​​).

The dependence on the squared deviation makes this a two-sided construction. We will not need to split the failure probability between upper and lower tails. Notice also that the tuning parameter has the units of VtV_tVt​, namely squared units of the observations, even though it is the precision of the mixing distribution.

4. Bound the probability of ever crossing

Let T=inf⁡{t≥1:Mt≥1/δ}T=\inf\{t\ge1:M_t\ge1/\delta\}T=inf{t≥1:Mt​≥1/δ}, with an infinite value if no crossing occurs. For every finite integer nnn, bounded optional stopping and nonnegativity give

Pr⁡(T≤n)δ≤E[MT∧n]≤1.\frac{\Pr(T\le n)}{\delta}\allowbreak\le\mathbb E[M_{T\wedge n}]\allowbreak\le1.δPr(T≤n)​≤E[MT∧n​]≤1.

Indeed, on the crossing event the stopped value is at least the threshold, and elsewhere it is nonnegative. The events increase as the horizon grows, so taking their probability limit proves

Pr⁡ ⁣(∃t≥1:Mt≥1/δ)≤δ.\Pr\!\left(\exists t\ge1:M_t\ge1/\delta\right)\allowbreak\le\delta.Pr(∃t≥1:Mt​≥1/δ)≤δ.

This is Ville's inequality. The proof uses only bounded stopping times; it does not assume eventual crossing or interchange an unbounded stopping time with an expectation.

5. Invert the boundary to obtain intervals

Outside an event of probability at most δ\deltaδ, the logarithm of the mixture remains below its threshold simultaneously at every time:

St22At−12log⁡(At/ρ)<log⁡(1/δ).\frac{S_t^2}{2A_t}-\frac12\log(A_t/\rho)\allowbreak<\log(1/\delta).2At​St2​​−21​log(At​/ρ)<log(1/δ).

For a compact expression, define

Bt=2log⁡(1/δ)+log⁡ ⁣(1+tσ2ρ).B_t=2\log(1/\delta)\allowbreak+\log\!\left(1+\frac{t\sigma^2}{\rho}\right).Bt​=2log(1/δ)+log(1+ρtσ2​).

Since St=t(Xˉt−μ)S_t=t(\bar X_t-\mu)St​=t(Xˉt​−μ), the interval radius and confidence sequence are

rt=(ρ+tσ2)Btt,r_t=\frac{\sqrt{(\rho+t\sigma^2)B_t}}{t},rt​=t(ρ+tσ2)Bt​​​,
Ct=[Xˉt−rt,Xˉt+rt],C_t=[\bar X_t-r_t,\bar X_t+r_t],Ct​=[Xˉt​−rt​,Xˉt​+rt​],
Pr⁡(∀t≥1:μ∈Ct)≥1−δ.\Pr(\forall t\ge1:\mu\in C_t)\allowbreak\ge1-\delta.Pr(∀t≥1:μ∈Ct​)≥1−δ.

Consequently, the interval retains its unconditional coverage at any almost surely finite data-dependent stopping time. This does not assert coverage conditional on deciding to stop.

For δ=0.05\delta=0.05δ=0.05, σ=1\sigma=1σ=1, and ρ=100\rho=100ρ=100, direct evaluation gives:

  • At t=100t=100t=100: radius 0.365639; full width 0.731279.
  • At t=1000t=1000t=1000: radius 0.096064; full width 0.192128.
  • At t=10000t=10000t=10000: radius 0.032730; full width 0.065460.

With all tuning parameters fixed, rt∼σlog⁡t/tr_t\sim\sigma\sqrt{\log t/t}rt​∼σlogt/t​. This construction does not attain the sharper iterated-logarithm rate. Choosing a tuning parameter in advance changes where the boundary is relatively tight, without changing its simultaneous guarantee.

What this permits in model evaluation

Freeze a predictor and evaluate its loss on fresh independent examples. Losses bounded in [0,1][0,1][0,1] satisfy the conditional moment bound with σ2=1/4\sigma^2=1/4σ2=1/4. We can inspect the resulting interval after every example and stop when its precision is sufficient. The guarantee concerns the fixed predictor's expected loss under the evaluation distribution.

For an A/B comparison, separate valid arm-wise confidence sequences, with failure budgets summing to the desired total, give simultaneous bounds on the difference of the arm means. Each arm must satisfy its own sampling and moment assumptions.

Stopping validity does not repair biased sampling. Changing the predictor after observing evaluation outcomes, selectively choosing examples, or replacing the known variance proxy with an empirical variance requires a new argument. Adaptive choices are allowed only insofar as the stated conditional assumptions continue to hold.
