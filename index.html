Mohammad Pouya Toroghi

M.Sc. student in Computer Engineering · Sharif University of Technology

My interests lie in the mathematical foundations of machine learning: how learning systems represent uncertainty, make decisions, and reason through unfamiliar problems. I am a master's student at Sharif University of Technology, advised by Mohammad Hossein Rohban in the RIML Lab, with an undergraduate background in electrical engineering.

Mathematics is central to my preparation. I have designed homework for Linear Algebra and Engineering Mathematics, designed homework and exams for Signals & Systems, and served as Head Teaching Assistant for Deep Reinforcement Learning and Communication Systems. My studies and projects connect probabilistic inference, optimization, search, and representation learning.

My current research concerns backdoor detection in language models and semantic calibration after fine-tuning; my undergraduate thesis studied machine unlearning. Across these problems, I am interested in what can be inferred from available information, which assumptions make an approach valid, and how uncertainty and computational cost affect the conclusions.

PhD interests

I want to pursue research that combines mathematical analysis with careful experiments. My interests extend across learning theory and optimization, statistical inference, reinforcement learning, structured reasoning, generative models, and the reliability of adaptive systems. The connections between these fields matter to me: a question about confidence can become a question about decision-making, and a question about representation can change how a system searches.

My long-term ambition is to contribute to general intelligence. I want to understand how systems can acquire reusable abstractions, assess their own reasoning, and revise what they have learned. My long-term questions set out these ambitions and how I hope to begin working toward them.

I am seeking PhD opportunities beginning in Fall 2027.
