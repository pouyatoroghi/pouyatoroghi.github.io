Generalization through a change of measure

Learning theory · Expository derivation

Choosing a predictor using the training data introduces dependence that a fixed-predictor bound cannot ignore. A PAC-Bayes argument handles this by controlling one exponential moment under a fixed prior, then paying a relative-entropy cost to change the distribution over predictors.

Setting and guarantee

This is an expository derivation of a standard PAC-Bayes inequality. Its useful feature is a guarantee that holds simultaneously for every distribution over predictors, including distributions selected using the training data. The complexity term measures departure from a prior rather than simply counting parameters. Seeger (2002) develops the related PAC-Bayes and convex-duality perspective.

The argument in five steps

1. Control one predictor's exponential moment

For fixed hhh, each Xi=L(h)−ℓ(h,Zi)X_i=L(h)-\ell(h,Z_i)Xi​=L(h)−ℓ(h,Zi​) has mean zero and lies in an interval of length one. Hoeffding's lemma gives

EetXi≤et2/8.\mathbb E e^{tX_i}\le e^{t^2/8}.EetXi​≤et2/8.

The constant follows by differentiating the log moment-generating function: its second derivative is a variance under exponential tilting. Tilting preserves the support interval. The variance is bounded by the mean squared distance from that interval's midpoint, hence by 1/41/41/4. The log moment-generating function and its first derivative vanish at zero. Integrating the second-derivative bound twice proves the lemma. Independence therefore yields

Why the temperature must be fixed

Optimizing λ\lambdaλ after observing KL(Q∥P)\mathrm{KL}(Q\|P)KL(Q∥P) is not justified by this theorem. The event was constructed for one value fixed before the data; it was not proved to hold for every temperature simultaneously.

One valid alternative uses a predetermined countable grid {λj}j≥1\{\lambda_j\}_{j\ge1}{λj​}j≥1​, with positive weights wjw_jwj​ summing to one. Assigning failure probability δwj\delta w_jδwj​ to the bound at λj\lambda_jλj​ and taking a union bound yields simultaneous guarantees with

What the result certifies

A concrete use is a finite-data generalization certificate for a randomized classifier: on a fresh example, draw a predictor from the learned distribution independently of that example, and use its prediction. With zero-one loss, L(Q)L(Q)L(Q) is its expected error probability. The same argument covers other losses bounded between zero and one.

The bound does not automatically certify a deterministic majority vote or a mean-parameter model. A prior learned from separate independent data can be treated conditionally as fixed; reusing the training sample requires an additional argument. If empirical Gibbs risk is estimated by Monte Carlo, its estimation error also needs accounting. Large KL divergence can make the certificate uninformative even when observed predictive performance is good.
